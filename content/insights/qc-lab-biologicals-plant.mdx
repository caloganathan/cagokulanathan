A QC laboratory for a biologicals plant exists to release the specification you sell: viable count or spore count, purity, specified contaminants, moisture where the form needs it, and a shelf-life result. Start with the methods and the sampling points, then buy the equipment those methods require. Accreditation for a testing laboratory in this industry is generally an ISO/IEC 17025 conversation with NABL. ISO 15189 is a medical-laboratory standard. ## Equipment, in the order of the test

Buy for the tests, not for the brochure.

| Test | What the bench actually needs |
| --- | --- |
| Viable count or spore count | A clean place to pour or spread, calibrated pipettes, a incubator that holds the temperature you wrote down, and a dilution scheme that fits the label claim |
| Purity and specified contaminants | Selective plates or methods named in the specification, not "a microscope and experience" |
| Moisture | A method that matches the carrier. A moisture balance nobody has checked is a random number |
| Shelf life | A storage condition that matches the warehouse, and a calendar. The chamber is useless without the pull dates |
| Identity, when the file requires it | Access to the fingerprint or biochemical method your registration names. You may buy this outside. You may not forget it |

Autoclave capacity, a laminar bench or the equivalent you can keep clean, and a water source that is not the tea kettle come before a PCR machine. Dr. Gokul has watched plants buy molecular equipment while the dilution blanks were being made at a dirty sink.

## SOPs

Each SOP names the sampling point, the method, the person who may release, and what happens when the number fails. A fail that only produces a retest is not a quality system. It is a request for a kinder sample. Corrective action has to be allowed to say "the batch does not ship".

Operator training is part of the SOP, not a separate motivational day. Tamil-language delivery is available when the people at the bench will not use an English document at midnight.

## Accreditation path

1. Methods that match the specification.
2. Records that show the methods were followed.
3. An internal audit that finds the gaps while they are still cheap.
4. Closure of those gaps with evidence, not with a revised header on the same empty record.
5. Only then, an application to the accreditation body you actually need.

We will help with design, SOPs, the internal audit and non-conformity closure. We will not tell you that we have led your laboratory to NABL. That sentence is not confirmed for publication, and it would be the wrong sentence to put on a client anyway. Your name has to be on the certificate.
