## Why these two organisms show the problem quickly

Trichoderma grows fast and sporulates in a way that hides a low contaminant until the count method looks for it. Pseudomonas grows fast in a liquid and will use a carbon source your media did not intend to share. Both are produced in plants where the people who handle raw carrier, soil and drums also handle the seed flask. That layout is the whole story in a surprising number of units, including new units that spent the capital on the tank and not on the door between dirty and clean.

More Indian manufacturers are adding in-house fermentation capacity. More litres means more fittings, more shifts and more ways to contaminate a batch that was clean in the flask. Capacity is not the failure. An unwritten method at the new volume is the failure.

## The seven causes

### 1. The seed is already mixed

The production tank is innocent. The flask, or the previous production tank you used as seed, was not. We ask for the seed history before we ask for the steam chart. If you cannot name the passage number and the last purity plate, the seed is the first experiment, not the tank.

### 2. Sterilisation does not reach the load

A cycle copied from a smaller vessel, a probe that does not see the cold spot, or a filter housing that is outside the steam path will all "pass" a chart and fail a plate. Solid substrate is worse, because the middle of a tray is not the edge. SIP is a load, not a setpoint.

### 3. Air bypasses the filter

A wet filter, a housing gasket, or a valve that lets compressed air in downstream of the filter will contaminate a batch that was sterile at the end of SIP. The symptom is often a contaminant that shows up late, after aeration has been running.

### 4. Biofilm in a place CIP does not touch

Dead legs, sample valves, sight glasses and the underside of a lid gasket grow a film that a circulation loop never hits. The batch looks clean twice and fails the third time, which is when the film is thick enough to shed. A CIP that is only a pump and a caustic drum is not a CIP.

### 5. Process water

The media water, the water you use to rinse after CIP, and the water you use to make up inoculum are three different risks if they are not the same controlled source. A softener that grows a film will undo a careful SIP.

### 6. The carrier or the pack

Trichoderma in particular is blended onto a solid after a clean fermentation. The carrier, the blender and the pack can contaminate a spore that left the tray clean. If the contaminant is typical of soil or of stored talc, look downstream of the fermenter before you rebuild the steriliser.

### 7. The sample is the contaminant

A flame that does not get used, a bottle rinsed at a dirty sink, or a dilution blank that is past its date will condemn a good batch. When a state laboratory and your laboratory disagree, compare the method, the dilution and the sampling point before you compare egos. Dr. Gokul has seen plants chase a fermenter fault for a month because the plate was poured badly.

## What we do on site

The Plant Health Check walks seed, SIP, air, CIP, water, carrier and sampling, in that order, unless the plates already point somewhere else. You get a written ranking, not seven recommendations of equal length. The first change is the one that removes a route into the tank. Training sits at the end, so operators are trained on the method you will actually keep.

We do not add an antibiotic to "stabilise" a biological. If the culture only stays pure with one, the process is open, and the product story you tell a buyer is at risk.

A related approved example from fermentation work, not a contamination percentage: sporulation viscosity and foam issues were resolved in 200–1,000 L fermenters. Contamination and foam are different failures. They can share a plant. They should not share a single corrective action.
