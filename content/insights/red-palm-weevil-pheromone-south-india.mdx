## Where the technology fits

Coconut IPM already has cultural and chemical habits that are hard to shift. A pheromone trap earns its place when it detects adults earlier than a walk through the garden, or when a mass-trapping layout is actually serviced. It does not earn its place as a device that is hung in March and remembered in August.

Around Coimbatore and Pollachi the question arrives from coconut. In the Western Ghats it arrives from tea, coffee and arecanut, with different insects. In horticulture it arrives as fruit fly. We treat each of those as an application to design for: insect, climate, service interval, and who will walk the row. We do not treat them as case studies with yield numbers. We do not have an approved yield claim to put under them.

## Two beetles, two programmes

Red palm weevil trapping uses a pheromone lure, commonly with a food bait, in a physical trap. The adult has to be able to enter, and the trap has to be serviced before it becomes a breeding site of its own. That last point is not a slogan. A neglected weevil trap is a small, expensive problem.

Rhinoceros beetle is a different insect. The lure is different. Hanging a weevil dispenser because it was already in the store is a wasted round. If a plantation has both insects, the deployment design says so in two rows of a table, not in one "beetle trap" SKU.

Fruit flies in horticulture are a third design. The dispenser, the trap colour and the servicing have their own constraints. A coconut layout copied into a mango orchard is how a pilot fails and the technology gets the blame.

## Controlled release, in the climate you have

A wax or polymer matrix holds the active so it does not leave on the first hot day. Load, matrix, venting and temperature write the release curve. South Indian plantation temperatures are part of the design, not a footnote. A longevity test that lives in an air-conditioned laboratory and never sees a palm garden will approve a dispenser the field will empty.

Manufacturing is the second half. Our founder has taken a wax-matrix lure to 500 kg for ICT smart traps. A laboratory dip that cannot be poured, cooled and packed at that kind of scale is not yet a product your purchasing team can reorder. The sensitivity and alert figures from that programme — 70% trap sensitivity, 40% better early-detection alerts, three port sites — describe ports and smart traps. They are not a coconut result, and we will not let a brochure imply that they are.

## Sensors

If the trap carries a sensor, calibrate the sensor with the lure you will actually fill, at the replacement interval you will actually keep. The deployment design includes who receives the alert and what they do on the same day. An alert that lands in an inbox nobody owns is not IPM.

## Registration, briefly

Many semiochemical products are looked at under the insecticides framework. Whether your dispenser needs a CIB&RC registration depends on the active, the claim and the current guideline. We will read that guideline for the product in front of us. We will not declare, in an article, that pheromone lures as a class are exempt.
