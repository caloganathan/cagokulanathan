<p data-answer>
Section 9(3B) of the Insecticides Act, 1968 was a provisional registration. Section 9(3) is the regular registration. For biopesticide strains that are already registered, CIB&RC discontinued 9(3B) grants and extensions. The public notice was uploaded on 28 April 2020. Those applicants go to Section 9(3). For the same formulation of an already-registered strain, the simplified file cited in that decision is a DNA fingerprint report and an analytical test report. A new strain, or a different formulation, does not inherit that shortcut. Harmonised bio-pesticide guidelines were published on 23 May 2022. We do not promise a certificate or a month count.
</p>

- 9(3B) is not an available holding pattern for an already-registered strain.
- "Same formulation of an already-registered strain" is a narrow sentence. Read all four nouns.
- New strains still need the data the current guideline asks for.
- The draft Pesticides Management Bill does not replace this route yet.

## The difference, in one table

| Question | Section 9(3B) | Section 9(3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it was | Provisional registration, with extensions | Regular registration |
| Already-registered strain, same formulation, after the 28 Apr 2020 notice | Discontinued. Do not file here and do not seek a further extension on that basis | The route. Simplified file cited: DNA fingerprint and analytical test report |
| New strain | Not a reason to use a provisional certificate as a strategy | The full data set in the guideline that matches the product |
| Consortium | Do not assume the single-strain simplification applies | Use the 23 May 2022 harmonised guidelines, including the consortium provisions |
| Who decides | CIB&RC | CIB&RC |

The Registration Committee's reasoning, recorded before that public notice, was that simplified guidelines already existed for the same formulation of an already-registered strain, and that efficacy, safety and packaging data were not required in that narrow case. The provisional route was therefore closed for those products. The logic was "use the regular route, which is now shorter here". The logic was not "microbial pesticides no longer need data".

## What "already registered" has to mean

It means the strain is already on a 9(3) registration, and you can say whose registration that is. A strain your neighbour registered is not your strain unless the authorisation says so. A strain you isolated last year is a new strain, even if the species is famous. Trichoderma harzianum on a thousand labels is not one strain.

The DNA fingerprint is how you stop that argument. The 2020 decision points to a fingerprint report from NBAIM and an analytical test report from CIL for that simplified case. If your paperwork names a different laboratory, check the current guideline before you ship the sample. We will not invent an alternative laboratory to save a week.

## What the 23 May 2022 guidelines added

CIB&RC published harmonised bio-pesticide data guidelines on 23 May 2022. The public notice covered biopesticides and consortium products. A consortium is not "two strains in one can" as a marketing idea. It has rules on composition, the inventor or source of each strain, and undertakings about what else is in the can. Mixing a consortium checklist with a single-strain file produces a query. Mixing a single-strain shortcut with a new consortium produces a worse one.

## How long it takes

We will not give you a range that pretends to be a plan. Use three questions instead.

- Is the strain already registered, and is the formulation the same?
- Which study, if any, still sits on the critical path: fingerprint, analytical report, efficacy, toxicology, chemistry?
- What happens to the date if that study slips by one cropping season?

An already-registered strain with a clean fingerprint and a clean analytical report is a different project from a new Beauveria that has never been in a guideline table. Trade folklore that says "nine months" or "two years" for both is how companies miss a season and then blame the secretariat.

The revised draft Pesticides Management Bill, 2025, released for consultation in January 2026, proposes digital registration and mandatory accreditation of testing laboratories. It is not the Insecticides Act. Do not build this year's file on a portal that does not exist yet. Do prefer a testing laboratory that can already show its accreditation.
