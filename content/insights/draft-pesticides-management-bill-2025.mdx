The revised draft Pesticides Management Bill, 2025 is a proposal. The Department of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare released it for consultation in January 2026. A Press Information Bureau note of 7 January 2026 says the draft is meant to replace the Insecticides Act, 1968 and the Insecticides Rules, 1971, and it highlights digital methods and mandatory accreditation of testing laboratories. Comments were invited until 4 February 2026. None of that is the law you file under today. Biopesticide makers should keep using Section 9(3) as it stands, and they should stop hiring laboratories that cannot explain their accreditation.

## What is worth preparing for, without pretending it has happened

**Digital registration.** The PIB note describes the use of technology and digital methods to streamline processes. If your regulatory file lives in one person's inbox, a future portal will hurt you. A file that a second person can follow is useful under the current Act as well. Prepare the hygiene. Do not buy a software story that claims to be "PMB-ready" as if the schema were published law.

**Accredited testing laboratories.** The same note says the Bill provides for mandatory accreditation of testing laboratories so that farmers get quality pesticides. The draft is the reason to care. ISO/IEC 17025, through NABL, is the conversation testing labs already have. ISO 15189 is a medical-laboratory standard. Our founder, Dr. Gokulanathan, holds an internal-auditor certificate in it from 2020. It is not the accreditation to print on an agri-biologicals lab.

**Penalties and traceability.** The PIB note also mentions stricter control of spurious pesticides, higher penalties, and traceability. Those are reasons to keep batch records you would be willing to show. They are not a reason to rewrite your manufacturing licence around a clause number we are not going to pretend to teach from a draft.

## What does not change this season

Section 9(3) filings, the closure of 9(3B) for already-registered strains, DNA fingerprint and analytical-report requirements in that narrow case, and the 23 May 2022 bio-pesticide guidelines all sit under the current Act. A manager who pauses a 9(3) file "until the new Bill" is choosing delay. We will not recommend that.

Biostimulants remain under the Fertiliser (Control) Order, not under this Bill's future portal. Do not mix the two reform stories in one board slide.
