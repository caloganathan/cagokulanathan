Under Rule 18(3) of the Coastal Aquaculture Authority Rules, 2024, no aquaculture input shall be made available or used in coastal aquaculture without certification from the Authority, unless the Authority has specifically exempted it. Probiotics and microbial feed additives are inputs. The application the Rules provide is Form III, for a certificate of compliance. The Authority decides. We support strain selection, scale-up, the technical documents and an antibiotic-free QA system. We do not grant the certificate.

## What to prepare

This is a briefing, not a reprint of the Rules. Read the gazette text and the Authority's current guideline alongside it.

1. Classify the product the way Form III classifies it: probiotic, feed additive, or another category. Do not invent a category because the brand line is shorter.
2. Match the strain on the label to the strain in the production record. A file that names two different cultures will fail a careful read.
3. Describe the process well enough that a reviewer can see where a contaminant or an antibiotic would be excluded.
4. Assemble the quality system: in-house tests or a named outside laboratory, batch records, and the sentence "does not contain antibiotics" backed by a method.
5. For importers, add the agreement with the manufacturer and the health or sanitary papers the form asks for. Then still apply. Those papers do not replace the certificate.

The Rules, as published, set a fee of ten thousand rupees per product on the application, a scrutiny step, and a period of ninety days for grant or refusal. A certificate granted under the rule is valid for five years. Confirm every one of those points on the current text before you budget. Amendments happen, and this paragraph is not the gazette.
