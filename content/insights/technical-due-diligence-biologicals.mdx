Technical due diligence on an agri-biologicals company checks whether the science, the factory and the regulatory status support the story in the deck. The questions are: is the registration valid today, who owns the data, is the strain authorisation in the company's name, and can the plant repeat the batch? <KeyTakeaways>

- A provisional biostimulant registration is not a registration after 17 Jun 2025.
- Strain authorisation and data ownership are different from a trade name.
- Capacity without a contamination and shelf-life history is an incomplete number.
- A refused plant visit is a finding.

</KeyTakeaways>

## Checklist

| Item | What "pass" looks like | Typical red flag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product category | The claim matches one regime: biopesticide, biofertiliser, Schedule VI biostimulant, or CAA input | One label, two statutes, no file for either |
| Registration status | A current certificate, checked against the authority's record or the gazette | A provisional biostimulant certificate used after 17 Jun 2025 |
| Data ownership | The company can show it may use the studies | Studies belong to a founder who has left, or to a university with no licence |
| Strain | Authorisation and identity sit with the company | The species is named. The strain is not. The neighbour holds the registration |
| Plant | Batches repeat at the volume in the model | The model uses a flask yield and a fermenter that has not held a sterile run |
| Quality | Methods are written. Failures have a CAPA | Release is a signature and a microscope |
| Shelf life | The date on the pack has a study behind it | The date was chosen by the printer |
| Laboratory | The lab can do the test, and its accreditation status is known | The draft Bill's accreditation idea is being treated as already solved, or ignored |
| IP story | Patents and know-how are listed with owners | A slide says "proprietary strain" and the assignment is missing |

## How the two to three weeks are spent

Week one is documents: certificates, strain papers, batch records, complaints, and the cap table only so we know who is supposed to own the science. We do not audit the accounts. Week two is the plant, if you can get us in, and calls with the production and regulatory leads. The memo follows. If documents arrive late, the date moves. We would rather move the date than pretend a closed factory was walked.

Counsel reads the contracts. We will flag a technical schedule that describes a different strain from the one in the fermenter. We will not negotiate the price.
