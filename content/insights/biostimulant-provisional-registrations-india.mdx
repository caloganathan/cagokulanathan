## What changed on 17 Jun 2025?

The provisional route existed so manufacturers and importers could generate bio-efficacy, toxicity and chemistry data while a product moved towards Schedule VI. It was extended more than once. The Press Information Bureau has stated that it was not extended beyond 16 Jun 2025, and that provisional registrations stand invalid since 17 Jun 2025.

That sentence is the whole commercial change. A certificate your distributor still has in a file is not permission to manufacture, import, stock or sell. A purchase order signed in May 2025 does not revive it. Relabelling the same bottle as a "plant tonic" or a "growth promoter", while the composition and the claim are still those of a biostimulant, is not a clever reading of the Order. It is a labelling risk.

We do not publish a count of products that were on provisional certificates, or a count of products now on Schedule VI. The figures in circulation do not agree. Your question is narrower and answerable: is this composition, under this name and this claim, in a gazette notification that is still current?

## What Schedule VI is, and what it is not

Schedule VI was introduced with Clause 20C by notification S.O. 882(E) on 23 February 2021. It is the schedule of biostimulant products notified under the Fertiliser (Control) Order, 1985. Later notifications add and adjust entries. A slide from 2024 is not the schedule.

Schedule VI is not a style guide for labels. It is not satisfied by "our product is similar to a notified humic acid". Similarity is how query letters start. The composition you manufacture, the claim you print, and the entry in the notification have to be read together by someone who is willing to say "this is not the same product".

It is also not a biofertiliser specification and not a CIB&RC registration. If the bottle promises pest control, you may be in the Insecticides Act as well as, or instead of, the Fertiliser (Control) Order. Fix the claim before you commission a trial. A trial designed for the wrong statute is a season you do not get back.

## A 90-day action plan

The point of 90 days is to make decisions while stock, labels and distributor promises are still in your control. It is not a statutory deadline we have invented. If your season is closer than that, compress the same steps. Do not skip the status check to "save" a week.

| Days | Action | Done when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–15 | Freeze shipments that rely only on a provisional certificate. List every SKU, composition and claim. | Each SKU is marked notified, not notified, or unclear. |
| 1–15 | Pull the gazette notifications and the Department of Agriculture circulars, not a trade PDF. | The file contains the primary PDF or a link to it. |
| 16–40 | Drop claims the data cannot support. Separate biostimulant, biofertiliser and biopesticide wording. | Marketing and technical agree a single claim sheet. |
| 16–40 | Write the data gap: efficacy, toxicity, chemistry, and the shelf-life date on the pack. | Each gap has an owner and a reason it is in that order. |
| 41–70 | Commission only the studies on the critical path. Do not start with the cheapest test. | Protocols match the claim you kept. |
| 41–90 | Draft the technical file and the query answers you would expect. | A reviewer outside the team can follow the argument. |
| 71–90 | Tell distributors, in writing, which SKUs have stopped. | No one is selling "until the new label arrives". |

Days 1–15 are uncomfortable because they look like a sales problem. They are a legal-product problem. We would rather you argue with us about a stopped SKU than argue with an inspector about a warehouse.

Days 16–40 are where most brands waste money. They ask a laboratory for "the full package" before they decide which sentence on the label they are willing to delete. A deleted sentence is cheaper than a trial. It is also more honest.

Days 41–70 are for protocols. Identity and chemistry that are still moving should not sit underneath a finished field trial. If the composition changes after the trial, you have a story and no data.

Days 71–90 are for the file a regulator can read. A dossier is an argument with evidence attached. A folder of chromatograms is not a dossier.
