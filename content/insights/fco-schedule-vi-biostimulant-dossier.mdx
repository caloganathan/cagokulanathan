A biostimulant dossier in India has to support inclusion in Schedule VI of the Fertiliser (Control) Order, 1985. Clause 20C and Schedule VI arrived by S.O. 882(E) on 23 February 2021. The public prerequisites are bio-efficacy, toxicity and chemistry data. Shelf life has to support any date you print. The detailed protocol is the current guideline and the gazette, not a shorter list from a vendor. We turn that official list into a sequence for one composition. We do not guarantee that the product will be notified.

## What must the dossier prove?

It must prove that this product is the product you say it is, that the claim on the label is the claim the studies tested, and that the safety and chemistry packages match the guideline you are filing under. "Bio-efficacy, toxicity and chemistry" is the public description of that package. It is not yet a method number you can photocopy from this article onto a purchase order.

That limit is deliberate. Guidelines and gazette notifications move. A webpage that invents plot sizes, animal counts or a private scoring system will be wrong the quarter after it is written, and it will look more precise than the Order. When we scope a file, we sit with the current text and your composition, and the roadmap cites the clause we are answering.

Shelf life is the claim companies forget. If the pack says two years, the file needs a reason to believe the product still meets its specification at two years, in a climate like the one you sell into. A refrigerator study in a mild city does not describe a shop shelf in May.

## How we sequence the work

The sequence is there to stop you buying studies in the order a laboratory happens to have a slot.

1. Freeze the composition. A moving formula makes every earlier chromatogram decorative.
2. Freeze the claim. One primary effect, on the crops you will actually test. Extra sentences are extra studies.
3. Chemistry and identity, so the trial is of a defined product.
4. Efficacy, on the claim you kept.
5. Toxicity, as the guideline frames it for that category.
6. Shelf life against the date and the pack.
7. The label, rewritten to the data, not the other way round.
8. A query list written by someone who did not draft the file.

Step 8 is the one internal teams skip. They fall in love with the narrative. A query is just the narrative meeting a reader who was not in the meeting.

## What the dossier is not

It is not a CIB&RC Form for a microbial pesticide. It is not the biofertiliser specification for a nitrogen-fixing culture. It is not a Coastal Aquaculture Authority certificate. Those are different products and different desks. Bacillus on the label does not tell you which desk. The verb in the claim does: kills a fungus, fixes nitrogen, or improves tolerance to stress.

It is also not a place to hide a provisional certificate. After 17 Jun 2025 that certificate is not a chapter in the file. Mentioning it as if it were current evidence will not help you.

## A claims review before any farm trial

We ask for the pack shot, the current draft label, and the one sentence your founder will not give up. We test that sentence against what a study can actually show in a season. If the sentence needs five crops and three modes of action, the sentence changes. This is dull work. It is also where the money is.

Importers have a further question: which overseas studies can travel, and which must be repeated because the guideline, the crop or the formulation does not match. A foreign registration is a fact about another country. It is not Schedule VI.
